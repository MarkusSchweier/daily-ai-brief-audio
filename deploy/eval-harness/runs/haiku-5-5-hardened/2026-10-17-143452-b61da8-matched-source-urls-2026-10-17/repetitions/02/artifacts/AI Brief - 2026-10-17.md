## 🔬 Research & Models

### Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5 designs quantum circuits that beat published designs (October 16, 2026)

Anthropic gave Claude Mythos 5 plain-language specifications for eight quantum-circuit primitives, plus an automated evaluator that scores each design on qubits and gates, and reports that the model improved on the best published circuits across chemistry, optimization, simulation, number theory and numerical computing. The loop ran with no human input between rounds. The headline result, a reversible divsteps circuit for modular inversion with no stored branch history, took 53 hours of model work after a one-paragraph brief, and one variant uses 12% more qubits but 17x fewer gates than the smallest prior design. A second result rebuilds the decoder inside the DQI optimization algorithm around Berlekamp–Massey decoding; the gate count falls roughly 12x against Google's earlier construction, and the qubit reserve shrinks from a worst-case 665 to 19 after 403 million simulated decodings. These are resource estimates from computational checks, not runs on quantum hardware, and Anthropic says Claude was initially conservative and favored well-known methods. Accounts also differ on breadth: Unite.AI quotes the arXiv abstract as reporting lower qubit and gate counts in seven of eight tasks, while Anthropic's post describes improvements across all eight primitives. The arXiv paper itself was not fetched for this brief, so that discrepancy is unresolved here. Why it matters: circuit design is code-expressible and cheaply verifiable, which makes it a clean test of agentic optimization in scientific computing.

**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/designing-efficient-quantum-circuits) · [arXiv abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22367) · [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-says-claude-beat-best-published-quantum-circuit-designs/) · [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/17/anthropics-claude-designs-leaner-quantum-circuits-reducing-resources-needed-for-key-calculations/)

### Reflection AI releases Beam weights under Apache 2.0 (October 16, 2026)

Reflection published the weights for Beam on Hugging Face on October 16 under an Apache 2.0 license, in BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 formats with a speculative-decoding drafter. Beam is a sparse mixture-of-experts model with 501B total and 23B active parameters, text-only, with a 1M-token context window, built for coding, reasoning and long-horizon agent work; Reflection first introduced it on October 5. The company's pitch is efficiency rather than peak capability: it says Beam reaches GLM 5.2-level reasoning with three to four times less inference compute. A pre-release comparison cited by DataCamp puts Beam at 80.1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, against 88.3 for Kimi K3 and 90.6 for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, so on agentic coding it trails the leading open models. The launch names more than 40 partners and includes on-premises deployment through Dell and HPE. Benchmark figures are vendor-reported, and independent evaluation was still pending at release. Why it matters: a permissively licensed model at this scale gives enterprises a US-developed option they can self-host and modify, and if the token-efficiency claim holds, buyers will compare models on cost per finished task rather than leaderboard score alone.

**Sources:** [Reflection](https://reflection.ai/blog/beam-is-available) · [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/reflection-releases-beams-open-weights-under-apache-2-0-license/) · [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/reflection-ai-beam) · [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/reflections-beam-aims-to-undercut-chinese-open-models-with-fewer-tokens-not-cheaper-tokens)

### Modal raises $750M at a $15.75B valuation (October 14, 2026)

Modal Labs announced a $750 million Series D led by Accel at a $15.75 billion post-money valuation, with Kleiner Perkins joining and all existing investors participating. The company says its annualized run rate has passed $1 billion, up from about $300 million at its Series C five months earlier, with usage tripled and revenue up more than 15x year over year. The new capital is earmarked for multi-node training, reinforcement-learning and post-training products, and inference work. All figures are company-reported. Why it matters: the valuation is being priced on agent and RL compute, not only on model serving, which is the same layer the SPACE release below targets.

## 🛠️ Products, Tools & Releases

### Perplexity open-sources its SPACE agent sandbox (October 15, 2026)

Perplexity released SPACE, the sandbox platform behind its Computer product, as open source under Apache 2.0. Each agent task runs in its own Firecracker microVM, network access and platform credentials sit outside the guest, and snapshots let work pause, resume or branch. The security evidence comes from a red-team report Perplexity published on September 23. Nine models with root inside the guest made 108 attempts to escape to the host, and none succeeded. With partial network access, four models got past the egress policy in 11 of 54 runs, using forged DNS responses or routing through services that shared an allowed CDN address; after Perplexity added fixes, a retest produced no verified bypass. Perplexity says eight of the ten third-party sandboxes it tested were susceptible to similar network-policy bypasses, and it publicly reports nine of them, seven with at least one bypass. The company also says SPACE has handled millions of sandbox creations per day; that is a company-reported figure without an independent measurement. Why it matters: Perplexity's distinction between VM isolation and network confinement is the more transferable lesson, and the report itself warns that a clean escape test is not proof of isolation.

**Sources:** [Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/escaping-space-part-i) · [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/15/perplexity-ai-open-sources-its-secure-ai-agent-sandbox-space/) · [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/16/perplexity-open-sources-space-firecracker-microvm-sandbox-ai-agents/)

### White House Super Intelligence Force adopts its charter (October 15, 2026) — Follow-up: the White House Accord cited in the 2026-10-16 brief's METR item

The task force adopted its charter at its first official meeting on Thursday, October 15, opening a 120-day window to recommend how the government should oversee frontier AI. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton chairs the group, and the President's Council of Advisers on Science and Technology will assess AI risk across cybersecurity and critical infrastructure, biosecurity, and model control. Pentagon CTO Emil Michael will draft protocols for AI labs to report breaches and cyber incidents. The task force is also implementing the White House Accord on Super Intelligence that frontier-AI chief executives signed earlier this month. The charter does not explain how compliance would be enforced, and it is unclear whether the new reporting mechanisms would bind companies at all. Why it matters: the open question is whether a voluntary accord turns into binding incident-reporting and oversight duties, and the breach-reporting protocols are where that will first be tested.

**Sources:** [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/15/2026/white-houses-ai-task-force-gets-to-work) · [Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/16/white-house-super-intelligence-force-charter-120-days/)

### Meta's revised commercial terms would allow crawling of business-linked websites for AI training (October 15, 2026)

Meta's revised Commercial Terms take effect October 30, and a new clause authorizes Meta and anyone acting for it to run scrapers, bots and crawlers against domains and pages associated with a business's products or services, then use that content to "train" Meta products. Nieman Lab reports that three legal experts read the language as reaching news sites, and that it allows Meta to create test credentials. Meta says the clause consolidates existing crawling language, does not change how it crawls publishers, and leaves robots.txt controls in place. Business accounts cannot opt out ahead of the date, and the update tells any business that does not accept the terms to stop business use of Meta's products. Enforceability is untested; the legal experts see an argument that the clause would override robots.txt instructions, a reading Meta disputes. Why it matters: the clause would route AI training rights through a platform's business contract rather than a content license, which bypasses the licensing negotiations that publishers and AI developers have been pursuing.

**Sources:** [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/) · [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/metas-new-terms-let-it-crawl-business-websites-to-train-ai-from-oct-30/)

### Taiwan plans to add AI servers and advanced chips to its export-controlled list (October 16, 2026)

Taiwan's Minister of Economic Affairs, Kung Ming-hsin, told Bloomberg that the government plans to add AI servers and cutting-edge semiconductors to its controlled-items list, so that unauthorized shipments to China would become a criminal offense under the Foreign Trade Act. Forkast dates the initial announcement to October 12 and ties the move to commitments in the 2026 reciprocal trade agreement with Washington. Until now the controls have worked from a blacklist that names Huawei and SMIC, and the proposal would instead treat the whole Chinese market as restricted. The amendment, drafted by DPP legislator Chung Chia-pin, still has to pass Taiwan's legislature, and coverage reviewed for this brief gives no implementation date. Why it matters: it moves export enforcement from a licensing check toward criminal liability for the server and distribution channels that have carried restricted hardware into China.
