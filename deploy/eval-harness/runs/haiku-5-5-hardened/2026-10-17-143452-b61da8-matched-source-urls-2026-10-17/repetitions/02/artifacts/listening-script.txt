Your AI brief for Saturday, October 17, 2026.

Top story today: Anthropic says its model, Claude Mythos 5, redesigned quantum circuits largely on its own, and in several cases beat the best published designs. Quantum circuits are the recipes a quantum computer follows, and fewer qubits and fewer operations mean a useful machine could run an algorithm sooner. The company gave the model plain-language specifications for eight of these building blocks, plus an automated checker that scores each design. The headline result, a new way to perform modular inversion, took the model fifty-three hours of work with minimal human input. One variant uses twelve percent more qubits but seventeen times fewer gates than the smallest previous design. Anthropic says the model was conservative at first and tended to favor well-known methods. The results are estimates from computer checks, not runs on quantum hardware, and reports differ on whether the improvement covers all eight tasks or seven of them.

Reflection AI released the weights for Beam, its first open model. It is a 501-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts system with about 23 billion parameters active per token, released under the Apache 2.0 license, which allows commercial use and modification without special terms. Reflection says Beam reaches reasoning performance close to a rival open model while using three to four times less inference compute. On an agentic coding comparison Reflection itself published before release, Beam trails the leading open models, so its pitch is efficiency rather than peak capability. Those benchmark numbers are the company's own, and independent tests are still pending.

Anthropic's upcoming public listing may be valued lower than expected. According to a Reuters report published on October 15, investors who attended the company's investor day discussed a valuation of about one and a half trillion dollars, below the roughly two trillion dollars floated earlier. This follows the third-quarter results covered in the October fifteenth brief: sixteen point eight billion dollars in revenue, up forty-five percent from the prior quarter. Some investors were disappointed by the slower growth. Anthropic has not published a price or target of its own.

Modal, an AI infrastructure company, raised seven hundred fifty million dollars in a Series D round led by Accel, at a valuation of fifteen point seven five billion dollars. The company says its annualized revenue has passed one billion dollars, and its sandbox product, used to run code for AI agents, is on its own running at about three hundred million dollars a year.

Ramp, the finance software company, raised one point eight five billion dollars at a sixty billion dollar valuation.

Perplexity open-sourced SPACE, the sandbox it uses to contain AI agents, under the Apache 2.0 license. Each agent task runs in its own lightweight virtual machine, and credentials and network controls sit outside it. In its September red-team report, Perplexity tested nine models with root access inside the sandbox. None escaped the virtual machine in one hundred eight attempts. With partial network access, four models did get past the network rules in eleven of fifty-four runs, through DNS spoofing or by routing through services that shared an allowed address. After fixes, a retest found no verified bypass. Perplexity says eight of ten third-party sandboxes it tested had similar network weaknesses.

On policy, the White House Super Intelligence Force adopted its charter at its first official meeting on Thursday, October fifteenth. It has a one hundred twenty day window to recommend how the government should oversee frontier AI. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton chairs it. The group is also implementing the White House Accord on Super Intelligence that frontier AI chief executives signed earlier this month. The charter does not say how compliance would be enforced, and it is unclear whether the new incident-reporting rules would bind companies.

Meta's revised commercial terms take effect on October thirtieth. A new clause authorizes Meta to run crawlers against websites associated with a business's products or services and to use that content to train Meta's products. Three legal experts who reviewed the language told Nieman Lab it could be read as covering news sites. Meta says the clause only consolidates existing language and does not change how it crawls publishers, and that robots dot txt files still apply. Business accounts cannot opt out before the date. The experts see an argument that the clause overrides robots dot txt, which Meta disputes.

Finally, Taiwan plans to add AI servers and advanced semiconductors to its export-controlled list, which would make unauthorized shipments to China a criminal offense. Taiwan's economic affairs minister confirmed the plan to Bloomberg. The change is tied to commitments in Taiwan's trade agreement with Washington. The amendment still has to pass the legislature, and no start date has been set.
