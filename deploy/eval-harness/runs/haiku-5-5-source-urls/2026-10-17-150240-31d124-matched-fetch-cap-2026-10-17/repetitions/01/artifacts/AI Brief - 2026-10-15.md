## 🔬 Research & Models

### Google makes Gemini 4 Argon generally available (October 13, 2026)

Google put Gemini 4 Argon in front of developers and businesses worldwide on October 13, after a two-week preview restricted to cybersecurity partners and internal teams. Reuters frames the launch as an effort to close ground on OpenAI and Anthropic following months of delays and a leadership shake-up at DeepMind. Introductory API pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and the rate doubles after November 30. On the independent Vals AI index of professional work, Argon ranks first at 68.9%, 1.9 points ahead of Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Opus 5.5 at 67.0%. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it scores 53 at high reasoning, level with GPT-6 Astra at maximum effort. Cost is the sharper argument: at the promotional discount Artificial Analysis calculates $1.99 per index task, against $3.26 for Astra, but that rises to $3.98 once the discount ends. Google's own figures, including a more-than-35% rise in agentic code submissions among internal Antigravity users, remain vendor-reported. Why it matters: with the two labs roughly tied on the composite index, the contest is moving toward cost per completed task and long-output work, where Argon's 1-million-token output ceiling becomes relevant.

### Anthropic reports $16.8B in third-quarter revenue to prospective investors (October 14, 2026) — Follow-up: the OpenAI revenue-accounting story in the 2026-10-12 brief

Anthropic presented its third-quarter numbers at a closed-door investor session in San Francisco on October 14. Revenue came in at $16.8 billion, about 45% above the second quarter, when the company booked roughly $11.6 billion; first-quarter revenue was about $4.7 billion. A separate account puts the second-quarter base at $11.5 billion, so the prior-quarter figure is reported slightly differently across outlets. The company also told the room it was profitable on an adjusted basis and cash-flow positive in the quarter. The disclosure follows last week's semiconductor sell-off, which was partly blamed on confusion over OpenAI's restated run-rate of $50 billion, down from the roughly $70 billion widely reported earlier. It remains unclear whether Anthropic counts partner-channel sales the same way. Reuters has reported that the public listing is likely to slip past the US midterm elections, and the company is targeting a valuation above $2 trillion. Why it matters: if the two labs' revenue figures end up on a common basis, the IPO process could become the first like-for-like comparison between them.

### Memory-chip stocks lead a global semiconductor sell-off (October 14–15, 2026)

Chip stocks fell across Asia on Thursday after a weak Wall Street session. SK Hynix is now 57% below its peak of four months ago. The Kospi fell as much as 7.9%, and SK Hynix and Samsung each dropped more than 9%. Hotter-than-expected US inflation data sharpened fears that the Federal Reserve will raise rates again, and the Nasdaq Composite closed 1.88% lower on Wednesday. Investors also reassessed what it costs to finance AI infrastructure. A second trigger was a claimed DRAM design breakthrough from China's CXMT, which is targeting a new server memory product by the end of 2026. Analysts called the reaction disproportionate, noting that CXMT has not shown large-scale production and that high-bandwidth memory is still dominated by Korean suppliers. The iShares semiconductor ETF, up more than 70% this year, has lost money in each of the past seven sessions, and CoreWeave fell more than 15% after a chart of its bond yields circulated online. Why it matters: the sell-off ties chip valuations to the cost of capital and to whether AI spending can be financed, rather than to any single product, which makes memory suppliers a read-through for the funding debate.

### Judge grants Apple expedited access to OpenAI hiring materials (October 13, 2026)

U.S. District Judge Edward Davila allowed Apple to see interview materials from OpenAI's dealings with former Apple employees who applied to its consumer hardware and robotics groups. OpenAI has five days to produce material it has already collected and 14 days for anything else covered by the request. The order also lets Apple view private LINE messages between named employees, and it approves depositions of Chang Liu, Alyssa Peng and OpenAI's chief hardware officer, Tang Yew Tan, all of whom previously worked at Apple. Davila refused expedited access to OpenAI research on areas such as metal finishes and power management, and he rejected broader hardware discovery, though Apple may narrow those requests and refile. Apple's July lawsuit alleges that former employees downloaded confidential files on Apple laptops and coached recruits on what to bring to interviews. Coverage I retrieved does not include OpenAI's response to the order. Why it matters: the dispute centres on recruitment records as much as on code, so interview materials are becoming a discovery target for any hardware or robotics team that hires from a rival, and OpenAI's move into consumer devices raises the stakes.

## 🏛️ Policy, Safety & Society

### Google DeepMind and GovTech Singapore build an on-device scam detector (October 14, 2026)

Google DeepMind and Singapore's Government Technology Agency (GovTech) have built a scam detector that runs on the phone rather than on a server. The team fine-tuned a lightweight Gemma model on local scam conversations, including impersonations of public institutions, banks and local figures. Detection runs in two stages: a light classifier screens incoming messages, and the fine-tuned Gemma model then reviews a longer snapshot of the conversation to confirm or dismiss a threat. When it flags manipulation, the phone shows a warning urging the user to pause before sharing information or money. The partners say the model will only read conversations with user consent, and that neither Google nor the Singapore government will be able to see them. Scams account for more than half of Singapore's reported crimes and more than S$4 billion in losses since 2019, and social engineering drives up to 80% of those losses. The companies have not published false-positive rates, accuracy benchmarks, supported languages, device requirements or a deployment timetable. The work was presented at Singapore International Cyber Week 2026 and sits within Google's National AI Partnership with Singapore. Why it matters: it is a design template for end-to-end encrypted messaging, where a platform cannot inspect chats on its servers and classification moves onto the handset instead.
